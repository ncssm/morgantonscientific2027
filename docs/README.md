# Morganton Scientific — Editorial Guides

Three role guides, plus the plan for how this volume runs. The guides were Written after preparing Volume 3, and
grounded in what actually needed fixing that year rather than general advice.

| Guide | For | Covers |
|---|---|---|
| [Author's Guide](author-guide.md) | Student authors | What to supply and why: ORCID, CRediT roles, abbreviations, corresponding author, figures vs tables vs charts, citations, abstract, and a pre-submission checklist |
| [Copy Editor's Checklist](copy-editor-checklist.md) | Content review | Completeness checks, figure and table handling, author metadata, and the house style guide |
| [Layout Editor's Guide](layout-editor-guide.md) | MyST conversion | `myst.yml` and `article.md` structure, cross-references, citations, verification, and the Volume 3 mistake table |
| [Volume 4 workflow](volume-4-workflow.md) | Everyone | How this volume gets made, intake through publication |

## Why these exist

Preparing Volume 3 surfaced the same handful of problems again and again across fifteen
submissions. Almost all of them were faster to prevent than to fix:

- **36 of 84** figures and tables were never mentioned in the prose; three papers referenced none of
  their own
- **60** bibliography entries across 10 articles were never cited
- **0 of 15** submissions arrived with CRediT roles — which the venue requires, and which became the
  last item holding up the volume
- Three tables arrived as screenshots, and had to be transcribed by hand from low-resolution images
- Two papers had every figure reference off by one

None of these are technical problems. They're all things an author can settle in minutes while the
work is still fresh, or a copy editor can catch in a single read.

## Using them

The author guide is the one to circulate widely — ideally at the point students start writing, not
when they submit. It's written to be readable by someone who has never heard of MyST, and assumes
they'll work in Google Docs or Word.

The other two are internal. The layout editor guide in particular is a working reference rather than
something to read end to end.

## Keeping them current

These are markdown so they can be version-controlled alongside the volume and diffed year to year.
If they move to a Google Doc or the journal website, keep these as the source of truth and export
from here — otherwise they drift.

After each volume, revisit the mistake table in the layout editor guide. Anything that recurs is a
candidate for the author guide, since preventing it upstream is always cheaper.
